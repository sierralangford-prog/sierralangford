# Make the portfolio evergreen and capability-led

## Direction
Keep the website focused on proof of work rather than duplicating a fixed resume. Preserve company context, role ownership, measurable outcomes, named events, and dated source materials when the date is part of the real artifact.

## Changes
- Replace the date-heavy Career Timeline with a compact **Experience** page organized around professional scope, organizations, roles, and linked work—not month-by-month chronology.
- Rename **Timeline** to **Experience** in navigation and page language.
- Remove date ranges from project cards and project-page headers/details so visitors scan category, organization, role, industry, challenge, and results first.
- Simplify the web Resume page by removing its full chronological role list. Keep a concise summary, capabilities, education, certifications, current downloadable resume, and strong links back to portfolio proof.
- Remove unnecessary “since 2017” language from general biography copy while keeping durable evidence of substantial experience and entrepreneurship.
- Keep meaningful names such as “Connection 2026,” “HIMSS 2026,” award artwork, publication titles, and source links unchanged because those dates identify real work rather than impose a resume chronology.
- Keep the Work archive grouped by type of work as the primary browsing structure.

## Technical details
- Retain existing structured dates in project and career data for future editing and source accuracy, but stop rendering them in evergreen portfolio views.
- Preserve the existing `/timeline` address so old links continue to work; only its visible purpose changes to Experience.
- Update page titles and descriptions to match the new evergreen framing.

## Verification
- Check Home, Work, a project page, Experience, Resume, About, header, and footer on desktop and mobile.
- Confirm project browsing remains category-led, credibility details remain clear, and no prominent date ranges remain outside intentionally named work or source material.
